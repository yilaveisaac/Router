How to read the table

  • The distance is measured along roads, not in a straight line, so it is longer than the gap you would see on a plain map.
  • The time is a best case. See how travel time is calculated for what it leaves out.
  • Starting and ending points matter. A trip from the far side of a large city to the far side of another can differ noticeably from the centre-to-centre figure.

What makes real journeys longer

  • Traffic at the city edges. The first and last stretch of an intercity trip often take longest.
  • Road condition. Damaged surfaces, flooding in the rainy season and roadworks slow you down.
  • Queues. Toll points, checkpoints and busy junctions add time.
  • Stops. Fuel, meals and rest breaks are not in the totals.
  • Time of day. Leaving early usually beats leaving at the peak.

Using the figures

  • Add a buffer to the driving time before you set an arrival time for someone waiting for you.
  • Use the distance to estimate your fuel. See how to estimate fuel cost for a road trip.
  • For any other pair of places, enter your own destination in Geo Router and read the result from your current location.
