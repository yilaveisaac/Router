A long drive goes better when the planning is done before you leave. Use this checklist as a starting point and adjust it for your vehicle and route.

Plan the route

  • Check the distance and driving time in Geo Router, then add a generous buffer. See why the time is a best case.
  • Look up current road and security conditions for your route from trusted local sources before you go.
  • Choose your departure time to avoid city rush hours and to finish well before dark.
  • Plan fuel and rest stops in advance, particularly on stretches where towns are far apart.
  • Tell someone your route and expected arrival time.

Check the vehicle

  • Tyre pressure on all four tyres, plus the spare, and the condition of the tread.
  • Engine oil, coolant and brake fluid levels.
  • Battery, lights, indicators and wipers.
  • Brakes: if they feel soft or noisy, have them checked before a long trip.
  • Jack, wheel spanner and a working spare tyre.

Carry the right documents

Requirements can change, so confirm the current list with the Federal Road Safety Corps. These are commonly asked for:

  • your driver's licence
  • vehicle registration and proof of ownership
  • valid insurance
  • a valid road-worthiness certificate

Pack for the road

  • Water and snacks.
  • A first-aid kit and any medication you need.
  • A torch, a reflective warning triangle and basic tools.
  • A phone charger for the car and a power bank.
  • Cash as well as bank transfer options, since networks and card machines are not always reliable.

Get your phone ready

  • Allow location for your browser, and see what to do if location is not detected.
  • Charge your phone fully, and keep it where you can see the map without handling it while driving.
  • Save important numbers, such as someone at your destination, offline.

On the road

  • Refuel before the tank gets low, not after.
  • Take a break about every two hours. Tiredness is a common cause of accidents.
  • Keep to the speed limit and leave extra space in wet weather.
  • If the journey is long, share the driving where you can.

Estimate your budget

Fuel, tolls, food and parking add up on long trips. Work out your fuel with our guide to estimating fuel cost for a road trip, then add tolls and a small reserve for the unexpected.
